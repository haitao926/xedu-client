# PBL Course Design

Use this reference when the request is about a project-based learning course, project-centered unit, public product, real user/community problem, or redesign from disconnected activities into a project.

## What Counts As PBL

PBL is a curriculum-and-instruction model in which the project carries the central learning goals. It is not a loose bundle of activities, a tool tour, a final demonstration, or a product added after the content is already taught. Before calling a unit PBL, name the curriculum basis or central learning goals and show how the project requires students to use them. If that mapping is absent, record it as pending and use the lower-scope label until it is resolved.

PBLWorks' Gold Standard model identifies seven essential project design elements: a challenging problem or question, sustained inquiry, authenticity, student voice and choice, reflection, critique and revision, and a public product. Treat these as design evidence requirements, not labels to paste onto a lesson plan. This skill additionally requires curriculum traceability and process evidence as delivery safeguards; they are not presented as extra PBLWorks elements.

## Choose The Honest Scope

| Scope | Minimum claim | Typical evidence | Do not call it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `learning_sequence` | Learners develop knowledge or a component skill. | Practice, explanation, diagnostic/formative checks. | PBL because it uses an interesting tool or produces an artifact. |
| `project_task` | Learners frame a bounded problem, make/test a partial solution, and explain a design decision. | Project brief, prototype/component test, feedback, revision note. | A complete PBL unit when inquiry, authenticity, revision, or public audience is absent. |
| `PBL_unit` | A project is the vehicle for core learning and all seven elements are demonstrably designed. | Inquiry trail, drafts, critique/revision trail, reflection, and an external public product. | PBL merely because groups build or present something. |

Time is a constraint, not a label. A two-lesson experience may be one phase of a larger PBL unit, or it may be a useful `project_task`. Do not manufacture a full-unit claim unless the surrounding inquiry, feedback, revision, and audience are already real and explicitly in scope.

## Project Charter Before Lessons

Make these decisions before writing a lesson sequence:

| Charter field | Design question | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem/question | What user-relevant problem, decision, or question organizes the work? | A concise driving question and scope boundary. |
| User/audience | Who has a reason to care, use, judge, or be affected? | Confirmed partner or clearly labeled prospective user plus validation plan. |
| Context and value | Where would the solution be used, and what change would count as useful? | User scenario, need evidence, or disciplinary quality standard. |
| Product/action | What will students make, recommend, test, explain, or change? | Deliverable definition and non-goals. |
| Learning core | Which knowledge and skills are indispensable for a defensible result? | Objectives and individual evidence plan. |
| Constraints | What time, equipment, data, safety, privacy, accessibility, and permission limits apply? | Feasibility check and contingency. |
| Student decisions | Which decisions can students own, and what trade-offs constrain them? | Decision log prompts, not cosmetic choice. |
| Success and evidence | What would count as a useful, accurate, safe, and well-explained result? | Criteria, test plan, and feedback plan. |
| Public sharing | Who will see the work beyond the class, in what format, and what role will they play? | Audience invitation/route, protocol, or honest downgrade of the scope. |

Keep a fact/choice/assumption log from the charter onward. Mark each important statement as one of: `fact` (supplied and, where relevant, sourced), `course-design choice` (a deliberate teacher decision), or `pending assumption` (with validation action, owner, and latest useful time). If curriculum standards or required learning outcomes are not supplied, list them as pending mapping; do not invent an alignment. Do not invent a real partnership: a hypothetical user can support an authentic design exercise, but it is not equivalent to confirmed external user feedback or a public product.

## Readiness Audit

For every PBL element, record `status (met/partial/missing) -> design answer -> observable evidence -> feasibility risk -> mitigation -> owner -> latest useful time`. A PBL unit is not ready until all entries are defensible and `met`; otherwise state the missing conditions and use the honest lower-scope label. The audit must also establish that the project, rather than an unrelated lecture sequence, is the vehicle for the named core learning goals.

### 1. Challenging Problem Or Question

The driving question should open investigation and trade-offs while remaining bounded enough for the learners, time, and curriculum. "Build an app/device/model" is a task instruction, not a driving question. Better questions connect a user need, a quality threshold, and a decision, such as "How might we help [user] make [context] safer/more accessible while avoiding [constraint]?"

### 2. Sustained Inquiry

Students must generate or refine need-to-know questions and then use credible sources, data, observations, artifacts, or experts. The inquiry matters only when it changes a criterion, rule, design choice, model, or test. A sequence of teacher explanations and prescribed steps is instruction; it becomes PBL only when learners use instruction to advance the inquiry.

Maintain an inquiry trail for the major questions:

`need-to-know question -> source/data/observation -> finding -> design/test decision changed -> student artifact -> teacher response`

Orientation materials may build background knowledge, but do not count as inquiry unless this trail shows what changed.

### 3. Authenticity

Authenticity can arise from real-world context, task/tool/quality standards, real impact, or learners' personal concerns. Make the basis explicit. Do not use vague phrases such as "solves a real problem" without naming a context, affected person, and observable value.

### 4. Student Voice And Choice

Give choice over a consequential decision: which need to prioritize, which evidence to trust, which success criterion matters, what solution path to test, how to handle a trade-off, or how to communicate to a specific audience. Bound choices with common learning goals and transparent quality criteria. Color, font, grouping, or title choices alone do not count.

### 5. Critique And Revision

Feedback occurs before finalization and comes from an informed peer, user, expert, or teacher using visible criteria. Each group records: `draft/prototype -> feedback -> decision -> revision -> resulting evidence`. Separate correction of a syntax error from revision of the reasoning, design, or product quality.

### 6. Reflection

Build reflections around evidence: what the learner now understands; which inquiry/design decision changed and why; which feedback they accepted/rejected; where evidence is still weak; and how their contribution affected the group. End-of-project satisfaction surveys are not enough.

### 7. Public Product

For a full PBL unit, share work with people beyond the immediate class or teacher and define their role: user test, critique, exhibition audience, decision-maker, or recipient. A class presentation is valuable but only counts as internal rehearsal unless external participants or a genuine public channel are present. Protect privacy, consent, and safety, especially for recorded demonstrations or learner data.

## Process Evidence And Response

During the project, gather diagnostic and formative evidence that changes teaching or learning while there is still time to act. For each checkpoint, specify:

`evidence elicited -> interpretation rule -> criteria-linked feedback -> next student action -> teacher adjustment`

This is process-oriented assessment: it helps students improve the current work and helps the teacher modify the next workshop, grouping, scaffold, or revision opportunity. A score or comment given only after final submission does not meet this condition.

## Technical And Resource Integrity

### Causal Chain

When a project claims to be an integrated solution, require an inspectable chain:

`real input -> processing/rule/model -> output -> user-relevant effect -> test evidence`

For each asset, identify:

`asset -> learning/project role -> actual input -> actual output -> connection evidence -> test -> limitation -> keep/repurpose/defer/archive`

Only call work integrated when the necessary flow exists and is tested. If a Notebook analyzes stored data, a Scratch project demonstrates a camera interaction, and hardware runs an independent LED script, those may be useful components but not a single working system. Label the relationship accurately and assess each component for the claim it can actually support.

### Impact Chain And Claim Boundary

For a social, behavioral, environmental, or policy project, map:

`current condition -> intervention/decision -> expected mechanism -> observable near-term indicator -> scope/limitation`

Separate the level of evidence being claimed:

- **Feasibility:** Can the proposed action or prototype be carried out under the stated conditions?
- **Usability or acceptance:** Can intended people understand, use, or accept it in the test setting?
- **Outcome or impact:** Did it change the intended condition, and within what bounded population and time?

A demonstration, brief pilot, or unaudited self-report supports only the narrow claim its method can support. Record the baseline or comparison available, sample and time window, major confounds, and the wording boundary. Do not claim causal, long-term, or population-wide impact without suitable evidence.

### Permissions, Care, And Partner Capacity

Before work involves people, media, personal data, health/safety claims, an external audience, or a community partner, make a participation and partner check. Address consent and any school approval; data minimization, storage, access, and deletion; safeguarding and accessibility; recording and quotation permissions; the partner's role, authority, availability, and feedback criteria; and a workable fallback if contact, data access, or deployment permission fails.

Avoid collecting data that the project does not need. Do not stigmatize a group, expose learners or participants through identifiable examples, or make unsupported health, safety, or behavior claims. A named partner is not proof of access to their time, data, or permission to implement an intervention.

### Tool Selection

Use HTML for phenomena/parameter observation, Scratch for stage-based visual-programming interactions, Jupyter for code/data/model exploration, and hardware for physical input/output only when each has a distinct necessary role. Existing materials are candidates, not a quota. A resource with no objective, evidence, or causal-chain link should be repurposed, deferred, or removed from the core path.

### Truthful Evaluation

Distinguish planned behavior, simulation, component demonstration, linked prototype, and real-context test. Include normal, boundary, and failure cases. A successful single demonstration proves a narrow claim only; it does not prove robustness, user value, or safety.

## Assessment Embedded In Project Work

Design the evidence system before activities. For every major project claim, map:

`claim -> artifact or test evidence -> success criteria -> reviewer/feedback source -> revision decision -> assessment use`

Assess both the shared result and individual learning. A group prototype may demonstrate collaboration, but each learner needs visible evidence of their reasoning, technical understanding, inquiry contribution, or explanation. Do not grade tool count, visual polish alone, or claims that the evidence cannot substantiate.

Useful evidence includes a problem framing brief, need-to-know question trail, source/data notes, decision log, annotated sketch or model, test protocol and results, critique/revision trail, individual reflection, and audience-facing explanation. Select only the artifacts that support actual learning claims; avoid paperwork for its own sake.

## Execution Architecture

Use this as a flexible sequence, not a fixed checklist:

1. Launch an authentic problem and elicit what learners need to know.
2. Investigate and teach prerequisite knowledge just in time.
3. Define success criteria, constraints, and meaningful student choices.
4. Design, build, model, or propose a first version.
5. Test with representative cases and collect evidence.
6. Critique with named criteria; revise from the feedback.
7. Reflect and share a public product with the planned audience.

For each phase write the learner action, teacher move, student artifact, evidence to examine, decision it informs, time/resources, and contingency. This is what turns a project description into a runnable course.

- [PBLWorks: Gold Standard PBL](https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design) describes the seven essential elements and centers projects on key knowledge, understanding, and success skills.
- [PBLWorks: Public Product](https://www.pblworks.org/blog/gold-standard-pbl-public-product) explains why work shared beyond the classroom creates an authentic audience and why experts can contribute during, not only after, the project.
- [Ministry of Education: Deepening Basic Education Curriculum and Teaching Reform](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202306/content_6884785.htm) calls for learner-centered, inquiry-based, practical, interdisciplinary, and process-oriented teaching and evaluation in the Chinese basic-education context.
- [Ministry of Education: Further Deepening Education and Teaching Reform and Improving Compulsory Education Quality](https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/moe_1777/moe_1778/201907/t20190708_389416.html) calls for heuristic, interactive, inquiry-based, research-oriented, project-based, and collaborative learning, together with process and developmental evaluation.
- [Ministry of Education: Compulsory Education Curriculum Plan and Curriculum Standards (2022 Edition)](https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A26/s8001/202204/t20220420_619921.html) establishes the current national curriculum-plan and standards basis for compulsory education implementation.
